## Brief: patent-landscape

### Key facts

- Fluorspar flotation art in the US is largely expired: foundational fatty-acid collector patents date to the 1960s–70s (US3430765A, acid-grade fluorspar via fatty acids/soaps, https://patents.google.com/patent/US3430765A/en; US4043455A, cationic-reagent flotation of apatite-bearing fluorspar, https://patents.google.com/patent/US4043455A/en). Recent filings are dominated by Chinese universities — e.g., US20230143136A1 (Wuhan University of Technology, pub. 2023-05-11), a sodium petroleum sulfonate + non-polar-oil low-temperature fluorite collector classified in B03D1/001, B03D1/02, B03D2203/04 (https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230143136A1/en).
- Core HF-from-fluorspar process art (C01B 7/19) is mature/expired: at least 14 US patents from 1919–1977 cover CaF2 + H2SO4 routes (list in US4120939 context, https://patents.justia.com/patents-by-us-classification/423/483?page=3); rotary-kiln operation is standardized at 200–250 °C, 30–60 min residence (https://www.neonickel.com/technical-resources/rotary-kilns-used-in-the-manufacture-of-hydrofluoric-acid-hf-2). The most recent notable US filing is Honeywell's US20070098624A1 on high-purity AHF from purified fluorspar (https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070098624A1/en).
- LiPF6 synthesis is being re-patented around HF-free routes: WO2019186481A1 (South African Nuclear Energy Corp, priority 2018-03-29) claims LiF + PF5 in inert perhalogenated solvent instead of anhydrous HF, CPC C01D15/005 (https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2019186481A1/en); WO2023094368A1 (Gujarat Fluorochemicals) claims high-purity LiPF6 (https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2023094368A1/en).
- LiFSI purification is a crowded, actively litigable niche: SES Holdings US10,926,190 (2021-02-23) and US10,967,295 (2021-04-06) on purifying crude LiFSI (impurities: LiF, LiCl, Li2SO4, LiFSO3, HF) (https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926190B2/en; https://patents.justia.com/patent/10967295); Arkema US11,316,200 (2022-04-26) on cold-crystallization drying/purification ≤25 °C (https://patents.justia.com/patent/11316200).
- Carbon Technology Holdings (fka Biogenic Reagents) holds a broad US biocarbon estate: US8,920,525; US10,167,437; US10,982,161; plus continuations US11,879,107 and US12,497,570 on "high-carbon biogenic reagents" claiming ≥70–95 wt% C dry basis and ≥14,500 Btu/lb products from inert-gas pyrolysis (https://patents.google.com/patent/US10167437B2/en; https://patents.justia.com/assignee/carbon-technology-holdings-llc).
- Activation + on-site energy integration is already claimed: US12,221,350 (Advanced Organic Methods Inc., granted 2025-02-11) covers continuous biomass thermolysis + pressurized steam char activation with co-production of hydrogen, steam, and electric power, CPC C01B32/30, C01B32/39 (https://patents.google.com/patent/US12221350B2/en).
- Calgon Carbon announced (2025-09-12) new PFAS patents: US 12,478,948 (reactivated carbon for PFAS removal), joining US 11,911,743 and US 12,319,593 (https://www.calgoncarbon.com/media/press-releases/2025-09-12/10624/).
- Kuraray dominates plant-derived hard carbon (H01M 4/587): US9,508,494 claims gas-phase halogen-compound demineralization of plant char at 500–1250 °C (https://patents.justia.com/assignee/kuraray-chemical-co-ltd); recent grants include US11,355,755 and US12,080,890 (2024) on plant-derived carbonaceous anode materials; commercial product is KURANODE (https://www.kuraray.com/global-en/products/kuranode/).
- Phillips 66 was granted US12,371,327 (2025-07-29) on nano-ordered carbon anodes for sodium-ion batteries from refinery hydrocarbon streams (https://patents.google.com/patent/US12371327B2/en); Stora Enso (Lignode, lignin hard carbon) files under Stora Enso OYJ (https://patents.justia.com/assignee/stora-enso-oyj) and partnered with Altris on Na-ion anodes in 2024 (https://www.ess-news.com/2024/06/06/altris-stora-enso-to-develop-sodium-ion-anode-material-from-lignin/).
- Cyclodextrin PFAS adsorbents (B01J 20) trace to Cornell/Dichtel: US9,624,314 (porous cyclodextrin polymeric materials), US10,882,023, US11,491,460, and US11,155,646 (charge-bearing CD polymers, 2021-10-26), exclusively licensed to Cyclopure (https://patents.google.com/patent/US9624314B2/en; https://patents.justia.com/patent/11155646).

### State of the art

Upstream (B03D flotation, C01B 7/19 HF) is old, thinly-filed-in-US territory: the operative US art on fluorspar flotation collectors and sulfuric-acid HF kilns expired decades ago, and new filings are overwhelmingly Chinese-origin collector/depressant chemistry entering via occasional US national-stage applications (US20230143136A1). Downstream is the opposite: electrolyte salts (C01B 25/455, C01D 15/005, H01M 10/0568) and PFAS adsorbents (B01J 20/26, C02F 1/28) are dense, recently-granted, litigation-grade estates held by SES, Arkema, Calgon, and Cyclopure/Cornell. Biocarbon sits in between: Carbon Technology Holdings has aggressively continued a 2011-priority family into 2024–25 grants with very broad composition claims on pyrolysis carbons, and US12,221,350 shows integrated "pyrolysis + activation + energy recovery" systems are now being claimed as systems — directly adjacent to the founder's plant concept. Hard carbon anodes (H01M 4/587) are crowded on plant-derived char with halogen-gas demineralization (Kuraray) and are attracting new entrants from adjacent feedstock bases (Phillips 66, Stora Enso).

### Gaps & pain points (candidate white space)

- **US-sited fluorspar beneficiation is near-empty white space.** Active US filings in B03D fluorite flotation are foreign-origin; nothing recent targets reprocessing legacy Illinois-Kentucky district tailings/gob, sensor-based fluorspar ore sorting, or coarse-particle flotation of CaF2 — strong novelty odds for site-specific process claims (foundation: expired US3430765A/US4043455A).
- **Integrated CaF2→HF→electrolyte-salt trains are unclaimed as systems.** HF kiln art is expired and salt art claims chemistry, not co-location: disclosures coupling biomass syngas/CHP heat to the HF rotary kiln, anhydrite (CaSO4) byproduct valorization with biochar, or HF off-gas capture into AlF3/synthetic cryolite would sit between C01B 7/19 and C01D/C01F with little recent art.
- **Fluoride-selective (defluoridation) adsorbents are sparse vs. PFAS.** B01J 20/C02F filing energy is concentrated on PFAS (Calgon, Cyclopure); Ca-loaded or CaF2-seeded biochar adsorbents for fluoride-laden mine/process water — regenerated back into the flotation feed — look thinly patented.
- **Crowded zones to design around, not into:** broad biogenic-carbon composition claims (Carbon Technology Holdings ≥70 wt% C family — an FTO priority), Kuraray's halogen-gas demineralization of plant char for anodes, LiFSI purification (SES/Arkema), and reactivated-GAC-for-PFAS (Calgon). For hard carbon, differentiation via hardwood-residue feedstock + on-site HF/fluoride ash-leaching integration must be drafted around Kuraray US9,508,494's gas-phase halogen demineralization claims.
- **Cross-stream coupling claims are the portfolio's best moat:** no located art combines pyrolysis waste heat/syngas with fluorochemical unit operations on one site; ~6–8 of the ~20 disclosures can plausibly target these interconnections (heat/mass integration, byproduct exchange), where examiner-citable prior art is thinnest.
