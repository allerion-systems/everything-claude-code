## Brief: energy-integration

### Key facts

- The core HF reaction (CaF2 + H2SO4 → CaSO4 + 2HF) is **strongly endothermic**, not exothermic: it runs in horizontal rotary kilns externally heated to 200–250 °C with 30–60 min residence time (https://www.neonickel.com/technical-resources/rotary-kilns-used-in-the-manufacture-of-hydrofluoric-acid-hf-2). The exothermic steps are upstream (H2SO4/oleum dilution and pre-reactor mixing), meaning an on-site pyrolysis heat surplus is a genuine complement to an HF kiln's heat demand.
- Patented HF-kiln heat-integration practice: EP1167286B1 (rotary-kiln HF process) preheats sulfuric acid to 80–180 °C by countercurrent contact with hot HF product gas, and divides the kiln into three indirectly fired zones with a declining temperature gradient (https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1167286B1/en; US counterpart US6,699,455, https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6699455).
- MECS (Elessent) HRS technology recovers sulfuric-acid heat of absorption/dilution as 4–10 barg steam, with ~90 references worldwide and ~30 years of operation — the standard for exothermic acid-side heat recovery (https://elessentct.com/technologies/mecs/technologiestechnologies-mecs-elessent-clean-technologies-mecs-processes/mecsr-hrstm-sulfuric-acid-heat-recovery-system-technology/; underlying patent US9,162,890, https://patents.justia.com/patent/9162890).
- PYREG's PX1500 carbonizer (screw reactor + patented FLOX flameless-oxidation combustor at up to 1000 °C; pyrolysis at 500–750 °C) delivers up to 4.8 GWh_th/yr of usable heat as hot water, steam, or thermal oil, and "when coupled to an external ORC module" can make the plant electricity-neutral or a net exporter (https://pyreg.com/technology).
- ElectraTherm markets ORC waste-heat-to-power (Power+ Generator / Power Module 75, twin-screw expander) specifically for biochar pyrolysis, citing 350–900 °C of otherwise-lost heat and "75–250 kW of power per unit"; in Nov 2025 it integrated its ORC models into Grain Ecosystems' biochar equipment-selection software including ITC modeling (https://electratherm.com/en/biochar-carbon-credits/; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/electratherms-orc-technology-integrated-into-grain-ecosystems-equipment-selection-platform-302602621.html).
- Heat-cascade precedent on hardwood residues: Metzler Forest Products (PA) runs a BET pyrolysis unit on sawmill waste and uses the heat to dry firewood, displacing 200,000+ gal LP gas/yr (~$500k/yr) (https://biochartoday.com/blog/industry-insights/biomass-energy-techniques-redefining-waste-to-value/).
- Small wood-gasification CHP is commercial: Spanner Re² HKA units span 30 kWe/73 kWth to ~68 kWe/144 kWth, with 550–700+ installations (https://bioenergyinternational.com/spanner-re%c2%b2-launches-new-generation-biomass-gasification-chp/; https://www.caluweinc.com/products/spanner-re2/); INNIO Jenbacher offers special-gas combustion systems for low/medium-LHV syngas, ~40% electrical efficiency on Type 2 engines and >90% total CHP efficiency (https://www.clarke-energy.com/2011/syngas-gas-engine-combustion-system-for-syngas/; https://www.clarke-energy.com/gas-engines/jenbacher-type-2-gas-engine/).
- Process-simulation literature reports ~80% combined efficiency for pyrolysis integrated with a CHP plant, with the CHP combustor drying feed and driving pyrolysis (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1876610219303467); intermediate-pyrolysis CHP techno-economics in Aston University's study (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306261917301186); fast pyrolysis + precedent steam drying integrated with municipal CHP (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267515803_Integration_of_biomass_fast_pyrolysis_and_precedent_feedstock_steam_drying_with_a_municipal_combined_heat_and_power_plant).
- Firebrick TES: Rondo's 100 MWh Heat Battery (California, Oct 2025) reached >1000 °C storage, >97% round-trip efficiency, steam at 100+ bar, charged solely by a 20 MW on-site PV array (https://www.rondo.com/news-press/rondo-powers-up-worlds-largest-industrial-heat-battery; https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/10/21/rondo-energys-100-mwh-heat-battery-powered-by-20-mw-of-onsite-solar/). Sand TES: Polar Night Energy's Pornainen unit (June 2025) is 1 MW/100 MWh using ~2,000 t crushed soapstone (https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/06/11/worlds-largest-1-mw-100-mwh-sand-battery-commissioned-in-finland/). Antora's 1800 °C carbon-block battery with TPV conversion is deployed at Wellhead Electric, CA (~1.5 MW module class) (https://howtostoreelectricity.com/antora-energy-thermal-battery-deep-dive/).
- Federal ITC: §48 "waste energy recovery property" (electricity solely from waste heat, ≤50 MW) required construction start before Jan 1, 2025; projects starting later fall under tech-neutral §48E clean electricity ITC (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/48; https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2736).
- Illinois: as of Jan 1, 2025 new net-metering customers get only supply + transmission credits for exports; ComEd/Ameren offer $300/kW-DC solar and $300/kWh storage rebates as partial compensation — strongly favoring behind-the-meter self-consumption sizing (https://www.solarinsure.com/net-metering-2-0-arrives-in-illinois; https://www.citizensutilityboard.org/blog/2024/07/01/cub-qa-whats-happening-to-solar-power-in-illinois-in-2025/).

### State of the art

Commercial biochar plants are heat-led, not power-led: the dominant configuration (PYREG, Biomass Controls) burns pyrolysis syngas immediately in a close-coupled FLOX/thermal-oxidizer stage and exports hot water/steam/thermal oil, avoiding tar-laden gas cleanup for engines (https://pyreg.com/technology). Engine-based syngas CHP is proven only where the gas is engine-grade — downdraft wood gasifiers (Spanner Re², Burkhardt, All Power Labs) at 25–70 kWe scale (https://www.caluweinc.com/products/spanner-re2/) or large plants using Jenbacher special-gas engines (https://www.clarke-energy.com/2011/syngas-gas-engine-combustion-system-for-syngas/). ORC bottoming (ElectraTherm) is the emerging standard for converting biochar-plant surplus heat to behind-the-meter power, now with vendor-integrated financial/ITC modeling (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/electratherms-orc-technology-integrated-into-grain-ecosystems-equipment-selection-platform-302602621.html). Pinch analysis is well established for pyrolysis-CHP-drying combinations (combined efficiencies ~80%; multi-stage flue-gas/steam drying studied for biomass power plants — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/B9780444634559500945), but published pinch studies address pyrolysis + power/district heat, not pyrolysis + mineral processing. On the acid side, exothermic-heat recovery (MECS HRS steam) and endothermic HF-kiln zone firing with product-gas acid preheat are separately mature (https://elessentct.com/...hrstm-sulfuric-acid-heat-recovery-system-technology/; https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1167286B1/en) — but no source found links them to biomass-derived heat. Electrified high-temperature TES (Rondo brick ~1000 °C/100+ bar steam; Polar Night sand; Antora carbon/TPV) is now operating at exactly the 1–20 MW scale relevant to a 40-acre park (https://www.rondo.com/news-press/rondo-powers-up-worlds-largest-industrial-heat-battery).

### Gaps & pain points (candidate white space)

1. **Pyrolysis-to-HF-kiln heat coupling is unclaimed.** No publication or patent found integrating biochar-plant FLOX exhaust/thermal oil with the endothermic fluorspar-H2SO4 rotary kiln (200–250 °C indirect duty — squarely within PYREG-class heat quality). Disclosure candidates: zoned kiln firing from carbonizer exhaust; syngas-fired HF kiln with biochar co-product; acid preheat via pyrolysis condenser heat.
2. **Exothermic-to-endothermic acid-side bridging**: routing H2SO4 dilution/absorption heat (MECS-HRS-style) to fluorspar concentrate/flotation-tailings drying or to ORC feed — published practice stops at steam export.
3. **TES-buffered heat dispatch** between a continuous kiln and variable pyrolysis/solar sources: US12,276,236 covers TES + cascade pyrolysis generally, but a sand/firebrick battery arbitrating among kiln, feedstock dryer, ORC, and post-2025 Illinois behind-the-meter economics (export credits gutted Jan 2025) appears open.
4. **Corrosion-tolerant recovery of low-grade HF-plant streams** (hot anhydrite/CaSO4 discharge, scrubbed tail gas) for biomass drying — no prior art surfaced.
5. **Small-scale pyrolysis-gas engine CHP** remains blocked by tar/low LHV (vendors burn the gas instead); a cleanup train yielding engine-grade gas plus activated-carbon co-product (using on-site char) is a plausible claim family.
6. **Regulatory timing**: waste-energy-recovery ITC under §48 lapsed for post-2024 construction starts; §48E qualification for ORC-on-pyrolysis and TES needs structuring — Grain/ElectraTherm's ITC modeling shows demand but no site-integrated method patents.
