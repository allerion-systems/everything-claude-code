## Concept 00

### CONCEPT: Legacy Tailings-to-Acidspar Retreat Circuit with XRT Gob Sorting, Sulfide Pre-Float for Ge, and Biochar Water Polish
**Pillar:** Fluorspar beneficiation / tailings reprocessing
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A four-stage retreat flowsheet for Illinois-Kentucky district legacy tailings and gob piles (feed ~15–40% CaF2 with 1–14% sphalerite, 1–5% galena, barite, calcite). (1) Scalp at 6 mm; +6 mm gob passes an XRT sensor sorter (dual-energy, 120–160 kV) ejecting quartz/limestone at >90% waste rejection before any grinding. (2) −6 mm fraction to a two-stage jig (0.5–1.5 Hz pulse) pulling a barite gravity pre-concentrate (SG 4.5 cut). (3) Jig middlings ground to P80 = 74 µm, then sulfide flotation at pH 8.5–9 with 50–100 g/t potassium amyl xanthate + 200 g/t CuSO4 activation, producing a saleable Ge-bearing sphalerite/galena concentrate (Ge deportment tracked by LA-ICP-MS assay gate at >150 ppm Ge in Zn con). (4) Fluorite rougher plus 5–7 column-cell cleaners: sodium oleate 300–600 g/t, acidized water glass (SiO2:Na2O modulus 2.8–3.2, acidified to pH 8) 1–2 kg/t with 200–400 g/t Al2(SO4)3 as calcite/celestite depressant, pulp 30–35 °C, wash-water bias 0.1–0.2 cm/s, yielding ≥97% CaF2 with <1.5% SiO2 and Pb <100 ppm. Process water exits through fixed beds of on-site Ca-loaded hardwood biochar capturing Ba, Cd, Pb (the ATSDR-documented contaminants) to <0.5 mg/L before full recycle; loaded biochar is returned to the sulfide float feed for metal recapture.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** US11478801B2 (jig + color sort + AWG/fulvic flowsheet for fluorite-barite ore, https://patents.google.com/patent/US11478801B2/en); TOMRA XRT at Las Cuevas (https://www.tomra.com/mining/media-center/news/2024/koura-flourspar-mine); AWG calcite depression (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892687513000745; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892687519301700); ATSDR Minerva No. 1 tailings metals (https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/MinervaMine1/MinervaMineHC050407.pdf); district Ge-in-sphalerite (https://www.uky.edu/KGS/news/2022_critical_mineral.php).
**Hypothesized white space:** US11478801B2 addresses run-of-mine ore with color sorting; no located art claims an integrated legacy-tailings retreat combining XRT gob sorting, a Ge-credited sulfide pre-float, AWG column cleaning to acidspar spec, and a closed water circuit whose heavy-metal sorbent (biochar) is recirculated into the sulfide float — i.e., a zero-discharge tailings-remediation-plus-acidspar system claim.
**Candidate CPC classes:** B03D 1/002; B03B 9/06; B07C 5/342; C02F 1/28
**Rosiclare site advantage:** The district's >125 years of tailings/gob (Minerva No. 1 ore was milled at Rosiclare until 1996) sit within trucking distance, and the pyrolysis pillar supplies the biochar sorbent at zero marginal logistics cost.

## Concept 01

### CONCEPT: Pyroligneous-Acid-Acidized Water Glass ("PA-AWG") Depressant for Low-Temperature Fluorite–Calcite Separation
**Pillar:** Fluorspar beneficiation (reagent manufacture from pyrolysis condensate)
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A flotation depressant made by acidifying sodium silicate (modulus 2.2–3.3, 25–40 wt% solution) with the aqueous condensate (pyroligneous acid, "wood vinegar") from on-site hardwood pyrolysis instead of mineral acid. The condensate — 4–8 wt% acetic acid, 0.5–2% formic acid, plus phenolics and humic/fulvic-like oligomers — is dosed at 0.3–0.8 kg condensate per kg silicate to a final pH 8–9, generating the chemisorbing H3SiO4⁻/H2SiO4²⁻ polysilicic species while co-delivering fulvic-type organics that reinforce calcite depression (mimicking the AWG + fulvic acid blend of US11478801B2 from a single waste-derived reagent). Composition claim: 60–85 wt% silicate solids, 10–30 wt% pyroligneous organics (of which ≥40% C2–C4 carboxylic acids), aged 2–24 h at 20–40 °C. Method claim: fluorite flotation at 15–25 °C (no pulp heating) using 0.8–2 kg/t PA-AWG with a 85:15–95:5 sodium oleate:sodium naphthenate collector blend at 250–500 g/t, achieving calcite depression equivalent to quebracho at ≥30 °C. The phenolic fraction additionally depresses residual carbonaceous slimes.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** AWG depressant chemistry (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892687513000745; https://www.mdpi.com/2075-163X/15/10/1020); US11478801B2 AWG/fulvic/naphthalene-sulfonate blend (https://patents.google.com/patent/US11478801B2/en); naphthenate low-temperature flotation (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271560207_Flotation_of_fluorite_from_ores_by_using_acidized_water_glass_as_depressant); bio-derived collectors US11638923 (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11638923); green acidspar flowsheets as stated goal (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652618307790).
**Hypothesized white space:** All located AWG art acidifies with H2SO4/HCl or adds fulvic acid as a separate purchased reagent; no art teaches biomass-pyrolysis condensate as the combined acidifier + organic co-depressant, nor the resulting defined silicate/carboxylic-acid/phenolic composition, nor its use to eliminate pulp heating.
**Candidate CPC classes:** B03D 1/006; B03D 1/008; B03D 2203/04; C10B 53/02
**Rosiclare site advantage:** Wood vinegar is an unavoidable co-product of the site's hardwood pyrolysis train, converting a disposal liability into the flotation circuit's principal modifier with no reagent freight.

## Concept 02

### CONCEPT: Flue-Gas CO2 Nanobubble Column Flotation of −20 µm Fluorite Slimes
**Pillar:** Tailings reprocessing / fines beneficiation, coupled to CHP flue gas
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A slimes-recovery process in which CO2-rich flue gas (8–14 vol% CO2) from the site's syngas CHP is water-scrubbed, compressed to 3–6 bar, dissolved in recycled process water, and released through venturi/membrane spargers to nucleate bulk nanobubbles (100–400 nm, ≥10⁸/mL) in a conditioning tank ahead of a 6–10 m flotation column. Feed is −20 µm fluorite slimes (tailings-pond fines, 20–35% solids). The dissolved CO2 self-buffers the pulp to pH 6.5–7.5, carbonating exposed calcite surfaces (converting reactive Ca sites to a hydrophilic carbonate layer) while fluorite floatability with 150–300 g/t sodium oleate is retained — allowing depressant dosage to be cut ≥50% and collector 40–60% versus conventional practice, consistent with reported nanobubble reagent savings and depressant-free microdispersion flotation of −20 µm fluorite. Claimed operating window: nanobubble gas holdup 0.5–2%, superficial gas velocity 0.8–1.5 cm/s in the column, wash-water ratio 1.1–1.3, two-stage cleaning to ≥95% CaF2 from 25–45% feed. Dependent claims cover CO2 mineralization credit (carbonate precipitated onto gangue reports to tails) and hybrid CO2/air bubble staging.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** Nanobubble conditioning (+~25% fine recovery, 50–60% reagent cut, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950555025000084); membrane microdispersion flotation of −20 µm fluorite with little/no depressant (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1383586620324552); column flotation of fluorspar byproduct streams (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42461-022-00666-1); fluorite tailings retreat reviews (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12613-025-3208-5).
**Hypothesized white space:** Located nanobubble art uses air and treats CO2 only as inert; no art teaches flue-gas-derived CO2 nanobubbles whose dissolution chemistry is itself the calcite-selective depression mechanism for fluorite fines, nor coupling a combustion source to flotation gas supply as a system.
**Candidate CPC classes:** B03D 1/24; B03D 1/14; B03D 1/006; C02F 1/24
**Rosiclare site advantage:** The CHP unit sits on the same 40 acres as the flotation plant, providing pressurized CO2-bearing flue gas and low-grade heat for the modest (25–30 °C) pulp tempering the fines circuit needs.

Additionally, published evidence that CO2 carbonates fluorite surfaces too (F−→CO3²⁻ exchange) creates an enablement/utility risk for the claimed calcite-only selectivity, so supporting selectivity data at the claimed pH window will likely be required.

## Concept 03

### CONCEPT: Continuous Twin-Screw Mechanofluorination of Acidspar to KF/NaF/LiF with Flue-Gas Regeneration of the CaTiO3 Sequestrant (HF-Free)
**Pillar:** Fluorochemicals (HF-free primary fluorination)
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A continuous, HF-free conversion of acid-grade CaF2 to alkali fluorides. Dried acidspar (≥97% CaF2, milled to −45 µm) is co-fed with KOH (or NaOH/LiOH·H2O) and anatase TiO2 at molar ratio CaF2:MOH:TiO2 = 1:2:(0.9–1.1) into a co-rotating twin-screw extruder (L/D 40–60, ≥3 kneading-block zones, barrel 150–250 °C supplied by pyrolysis waste heat, residence 2–10 min, specific mechanical energy 150–500 kWh/t), replacing the hours-long batch ball-milling of the published route with flow-mode shear activation. Products: 2 MF + CaTiO3, the perovskite sequestering Ca to block back-reaction exactly as in the JACS chemistry. Downstream: hot-water leach (KF/NaF dissolve; for LiF, a Na2CO3-free countercurrent wash exploits LiF's 1.3 g/L solubility with density separation from CaTiO3), crystallization to battery-precursor-grade MF. The regeneration loop is the second inventive layer: leach residue CaTiO3 is slurried and carbonated with CHP flue-gas CO2 at 10–20 bar, 80–150 °C, converting it to CaCO3 + regenerated TiO2; TiO2 is recycled to the extruder (target ≥90% Ti closure) and CaCO3 is sold or returned as flotation-circuit pH modifier, making TiO2 a true catalyst-like circulating agent rather than a stoichiometric consumable.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** One-step HF-free CaF2→LiF/NaF/KF with TiO2/CaTiO3, JACS 147, 6338 (2025) (https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jacs.4c16608; review https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.202500255); Fluoromix mechanochemistry, Science 381, 302 (2023) (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1557); FluoRok platform (https://www.fluorok.com/news/fluorok-raises-7-7m-9-8m-to-transform-the-safety-and-sustainability-of-fluorochemical-production/); reactive-extrusion scale-up of mechanochemistry (https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2022/cs/d1cs00657f).
**Hypothesized white space:** The Science/JACS/FluoRok art is batch ball-milling with stoichiometric, unrecovered TiO2; claimed here are (a) the continuous extrusion parameter window for this specific solid-state fluoride metathesis, (b) CO2-pressure carbonation regeneration of CaTiO3 back to TiO2 + CaCO3, and (c) the closed Ti/CO2/heat integration as a system.
**Candidate CPC classes:** C01D 3/02; C01D 15/04; B01J 19/20; C01F 11/18
**Rosiclare site advantage:** Extruder barrel heat and carbonation CO2 both come free from the co-located pyrolysis/CHP pillar, and the plant sits in the only US county (Hardin Co., with Cave-in-Rock stockpile sales) with current domestic fluorspar activity.

## Concept 04

### CONCEPT: Fully HF-Free Fluorspar-to-LiPF6 Train via KF Halex PF5 Generation and Perfluorocarbon-Medium Crystallization
**Pillar:** Fluorochemicals (HF-free electrolyte salt; US-owned LiPF6 process IP)
**Technical design / molecular structure:** An end-to-end LiPF6 route with no anhydrous HF anywhere. Step 1: KF and LiF are produced HF-free from acidspar by the site's mechanofluorination unit (CaF2 + KOH/LiOH + TiO2 → MF + CaTiO3). Step 2: PF5 generation by solid-phase halex — dried KF (5.0–5.5 mol) contacted with PCl5 (1.0 mol) in a heated screw/rotary reactor at 120–220 °C under 50–200 mbar with dry N2 sweep: 5 KF + PCl5 → PF5(g) + 5 KCl, displacing the industry-standard PCl5 + 5 HF route; PF5 is polished over NaF guard beds to strip POF3/HCl. Step 3: PF5 is sparged at 2–6 bar, 25–60 °C into a stirred suspension of the HF-free LiF in perfluorodecalin/perfluoroheptane, precipitating ≥99% LiPF6 that is inert-filtered, washed with anhydrous EMC, and vacuum-dried to ≤20 ppm H2O — adopting and improving the lapsed Necsa perfluorocarbon teaching with continuous loop-reactor operation and in-line Karl-Fischer/ion-chromatography release gates (target ≥99.9% purity, Cl⁻ <50 ppm, K <10 ppm). Step 4 (loop closure): KCl byproduct is membrane-electrolyzed to KOH (recycled to Step 1) and Cl2 (converted with purchased P4 to PCl3/PCl5), so fluorine flows CaF2→KF→PF5→LiPF6 and chlorine/potassium circulate internally.

## Concept 18

### CONCEPT: Self-Fluxing Bio-Reductant Briquette (Metspar–Biocarbon Composite for EAF/Ferroalloy Use)
**Pillar:** Coupled fluorspar beneficiation (a) + biochar pyrolysis (b) + co-located manufacturing (d)
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A cold-pressed, heat-cured composite briquette combining on-site metallurgical-grade fluorspar fines with engineered hardwood biocarbon, replacing both fluorspar lump and fossil carbon injectant in electric-arc steelmaking and Si/FeSi/FeMn furnaces. Composition claim ranges: 40–70 wt% metspar fines (≥80% CaF2, −200 mesh, sourced from flotation middlings and Cave-in-Rock byproduct stockpiles); 20–45 wt% hardwood-residue biochar pyrolyzed at 550–750 °C to fixed carbon ≥85%, ash ≤3%, volatile matter 5–12% (retained volatiles aid ignition and slag foaming); 5–15 wt% heavy pyrolysis tar/bio-oil fraction as binder; 0–8 wt% lignin or starch co-binder. In the EAF the briquette functions simultaneously as (i) slag fluidizer (CaF2 lowers liquidus of lime-rich slags), (ii) biogenic foaming/reducing carbon (charcoal's high CO2 gasification reactivity vs. coke), giving a single-addition "slag conditioner + carbon unit" with net-biogenic CO2. Dependent claims: dolomite/lime addition 2–10% to control S; Pb <100 ppm spec via upstream flotation.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** Biocarbon as Si/FeSi/FeMn reductant, charcoal reactivity advantage ([ACS Omega review](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.4c00866); [biocoke FeMn pilot](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166516225001089)); synthetic fluorspar slag conditioner ([US4053302A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4053302A/en)); commercial fluorspar briquettes use inorganic-salt binders only ([YST fluorspar briquette](https://www.ystfluorite.com/factory-customized-caf2-90-fluorspar-briquette-manufacture-product/)); BioCarbon Global markets loose biogenic reductant, not flux composites ([BioCarbon Global](https://www.biocarbonglobal.com/)).
**Hypothesized white space:** Existing fluorspar briquettes are CaF2-plus-inorganic-binder with no functional carbon; biocarbon reductant art is unformulated charcoal with no flux. No known art claims a single CaF2–biocarbon–pyrolysis-tar composite with cure-coked bio-binder and dual slag-conditioning/reductant function specs (strength, reactivity, CaF2:C ratio).
**Candidate CPC classes:** C22B1/244; C21C7/076; C10L5/447; C21C5/52
**Rosiclare site advantage:** Both feed streams (metspar fines from Hastie-type byproduct/tailings retreat and hardwood biochar plus tar binder) originate within the 40-acre site, and Smithland-pool Ohio River barge access reaches EAF mills at bulk freight rates.

**Technical design / molecular structure:** An engineered multilayer dry cover for IKFD legacy tailings (Ba/Cd/Pb-bearing, per ATSDR at Minerva No. 1) built from site reject streams. Layer 3: 15–30 cm growth medium (biochar 5–10 vol% + soil) for revegetation. Method claims: placing the cover over reprocessed (fluorite-depleted) tailings so remediation is performed with the reprocessing campaign's own reject streams; monitoring leachate F <4 mg/L (EPA MCL) and quantifying CO2 removal from biochar carbon plus rock-dust weathering.

## Concept 20

### CONCEPT: Fluorite-Seeded Activated Biochar Crystallization Media with Closed-Loop CaF2 Recovery
**Pillar:** Coupled biochar/activated carbon (b) + fluorochemical wastewater/scrubber circuit (a)+(d)
**Technical design / molecular structure:** A composition and water-treatment method in which steam-activated hardwood biochar (activated 800–900 °C, BET 600–900 m²/g, particle size 0.3–1.2 mm for fluidized-bed use) is impregnated with 10–25 wt% micronized acid-grade fluorite seed crystals (d50 1–5 µm, taken from on-site flotation concentrate fines) anchored in the macropore network, plus a surface calcium reservoir deposited from Ca(OH)2/CaCl2 (2–8 wt% Ca). In a fluidized pellet-reactor treating fluorochemical-plant scrubber blowdown and rinse waters (F⁻ 50–2,000 mg/L), lime is dosed at Ca/F molar ratio 0.50–0.60 while the seeded media circulates; fluoride is removed by heterogeneous crystallization of CaF2 onto the fluorite seed surfaces (avoiding gelatinous CaF2 sludge), polishing to <4 mg/L (EPA MCL) via residual adsorption on the carbon. Media is grown to >60 wt% CaF2, then withdrawn and routed either (i) directly into the acidspar flotation feed (fluorite reports to concentrate; carbon rejects to tails/fuel) or (ii) into the metspar–biocarbon briquette of the companion disclosure. Claims: the CaF2-seeded activated-biochar composite (composition ranges above); the crystallization-plus-adsorption method; and the closed-loop recycle of fluoride captured from effluent back into salable fluorspar product.
**Grounding prior art (from briefs):** La-modified biochars are the leading fluoride adsorbents (126.7 mg F/g) but create a spent-media waste and La cost/leaching issues ([La-coconut PVDF membrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39515139/); [PMC La-sludge](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11989919/)); Ca-only biochar is weak (0.09–0.51 mg F/g) ([PMC Ca-dairy-manure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970507/)); EPA fluoride MCL 4.0 mg/L ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/fluoride-drinking-water)); scrubber-media white space flagged for HF/fluoride off-gas capture (brief: biochar-materials gap #5).
**Hypothesized white space:** All modified-biochar fluoride media rely on metal-oxide adsorption sites and end as waste; none claim fluorite-seeded heterogeneous crystallization on a porous biocarbon carrier, and none claim regenerating captured fluoride as marketable CaF2 by feeding spent media back into a co-located beneficiation circuit.
**Candidate CPC classes:** C02F1/52; C02F2101/14; B01J20/20; C01F11/22
**Rosiclare site advantage:** Seed crystals, activated carbon, the fluoride-bearing effluent, and the flotation circuit that monetizes the loaded media all exist within one fence line, turning a hazardous-waste liability into acidspar yield.
