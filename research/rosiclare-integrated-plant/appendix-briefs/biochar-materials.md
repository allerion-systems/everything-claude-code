## Brief: biochar-materials

### Key facts

- **Biocarbon can substitute fossil coke/coal as a metallurgical reductant** in silicon, ferrosilicon, and manganese-alloy smelting; charcoals show significantly higher CO2 gasification reactivity than metallurgical coke, which is prized in Si/FeSi furnaces. Producers including Elkem participate in R&D (SINTEF/NTNU "BioMet," "Biocoke4FAI") to replace fossil carbon. [ACS Omega review](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.4c00866); [Springer Emerging Technologies for Decarbonizing Silicon](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40831-024-00913-3)
- Silicon-grade biocarbon requires **high fixed carbon, low ash, and controlled reactivity/mechanical strength**; BioCarbon Global markets biogenic charcoal ("high-fixed-carbon-content") specifically as a renewable reductant for silicon smelters. [BioCarbon Global](https://www.biocarbonglobal.com/)
- Bio-coke from **coconut shells** has been demonstrated as a drop-in metallurgical-coke replacement; biocoke was validated in a **ferromanganese smelting pilot furnace** via petrographic analysis. [Energy & Fuels](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.5c02366); [ScienceDirect biocoke FeMn pilot](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166516225001089)
- **Hard carbon is the dominant anode for sodium-ion batteries.** Stora Enso's lignin-derived **Lignode** (pilot plant, Kotka, Finland) is partnered with **Altris** (June 2024) to commercialize a sodium-ion anode. [Stora Enso newsroom](https://www.storaenso.com/en/newsroom/news/2024/6/partners-with-altris-sustainable-anode-material-sodium-ion-batteries); [ess-news](https://www.ess-news.com/2024/06/06/altris-stora-enso-to-develop-sodium-ion-anode-material-from-lignin/)
- **Kuraray KURANODE** is a coconut-shell-derived "BIOHARDCARBON" anode (Li-ion, Li-ion capacitors, and Na-ion); biomass hard carbons reach **~300 mAh/g with initial coulombic efficiency (ICE) >80%**. [Kuraray KURANODE](https://carbon-solutions.kuraray.com/en/kuranode/); [PMC synthesis review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566587/)
- **CFx (fluorinated carbon) is the highest-energy primary-battery cathode**: theoretical **865 mAh/g and 2180 Wh/kg at F/C = 1.0**. Fluorinated biomass/hard carbons beat commercial CFx: nut-shell-derived CFx reached **2585 Wh/kg**; gas-phase-fluorinated hard carbon at 390 °C hit **922.6 mAh/g, 2.7 V, 2466 Wh/kg**. [ScienceDirect fluorinated hard carbon](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2452213920301248); [Rare Metals N,P co-doped biomass CFx](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1007/s12598-024-02894-4)
- **Semi-ionic C–F bonds** (formed at lower fluorination temperature) preserve conductivity and give ~3.2 V plateaus and better rate capability vs. low-conductivity covalent C–F; F/C ratio and bond character are tunable by fluorination temperature — a core process lever. [ScienceDirect fluorinated hard carbon](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2452213920301248)
- **Lanthanum-modified biochar is the leading fluoride-adsorption media**: La-coconut-shell biochar **126.7 mg F/g** (vs. 27.4 bare); La-Sargassum **94.3 mg/g**; La-sludge biochar (La-SBC-700) **40.3 mg/g**; La-pomelo peel **19.86 mg/g**. Calcium-modified dairy-manure biochar is far weaker (**0.09–0.51 mg F/g**). [PMC La-sludge](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11989919/); [Springer La-pomelo](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11356-018-1727-6); [PMC Ca-dairy-manure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970507/); [La-coconut PVDF UF membrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39515139/)
- Regulatory driver: US EPA fluoride **MCL 4.0 mg/L** (secondary 2.0 mg/L for dental fluorosis); **WHO guideline 1.5 mg/L**. Geothermal/groundwater basins routinely exceed these. [EPA Fluoride in Drinking Water](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/fluoride-drinking-water); [PMC La-sludge geothermal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11989919/)
- **Carbon-negative concrete** with **30 wt% CO2-weathered biochar + 70% Portland-limestone cement** reached **27.6 MPa at 28 days**; WSU filed a provisional patent. Biochar 3D-printable concrete cut carbon footprint 8.3% at 2 wt%. [WSU/ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/04/230418101419.htm); [Nature Comms Materials 3D print](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43246-024-00700-3)
- **Biochar + basalt/silicate rock-dust co-application** (enhanced rock weathering) shows additive CO2 removal and nutrient release; biochar boosts porosity/hydrology to accelerate mineral weathering. [ScienceDirect additive effects](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883292724001598); [Frontiers biochar+basanite](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2026.1853116/full)

### State of the art

Metallurgical biocarbon is at pilot/pre-commercial scale in Europe (Norway's ferroalloy cluster) and demonstration scale for bio-coke from coconut/nut shells. The technical frontier is engineering fixed carbon (>85–90%), low ash/phosphorus, density, and briquette/pellet mechanical integrity so biochar survives furnace burden — properties native biochar lacks. Sodium-ion hard carbon is commercializing fast (Stora Enso/Altris, Kuraray, HiNa, Faradion supply chains); the differentiators are ICE, reversible capacity (~300–350 mAh/g target), and low-cost lignocellulosic precursors with controlled closed-pore microstructure. CFx is a mature primary-cathode chemistry (medical, defense, IoT), but biomass-derived CFx with semi-ionic bond engineering is early-stage academic work — a clear coupling point with an on-site fluorine/HF stream. Fluoride adsorption via La/Ca/Al-modified biochar is lab-to-pilot; La dominates performance but raises cost/leaching concerns. Biochar-concrete and biochar-rock-dust are demonstration-scale with provisional patents emerging.

### Gaps & pain points (candidate white space)

1. **Integrated biomass→CFx cathode**: no patent couples on-site fluorspar-derived HF/F2 with in-house hardwood-pyrolysis hard carbon to make CFx; claim biomass-derived carbon + temperature-controlled semi-ionic C–F bond ratio for >2400 Wh/kg. Strong site-synergy novelty. [Rare Metals](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1007/s12598-024-02894-4)
2. **CaF2 / fluorspar-tailings-modified biochar for fluoride adsorption** — a Ca-and-La-on-biochar composite made from on-site mineral by-products; white space vs. weak Ca-only and costly La-only media, with a closed-loop "fluoride-capture" angle. [PMC Ca-manure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970507/)
3. **Silicon-grade bio-reductant briquette** engineered from hardwood residue with a mineral (CaF2 flux) binder — couples streams (a)+(b); patent the fixed-carbon/reactivity/strength spec and flux integration. Existing art is generic charcoal; formulation + flux is open. [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.4c00866)
4. **Waste-heat/syngas-CHP-driven co-production** of hard-carbon (anode) + activated carbon (fluoride media) from one pyrolysis train — process-integration claims tying gasification off-gas to carbonization temperature control are thin.
5. **Fluorochemical-plant scrubber media**: biochar activated carbon tuned for HF/fluoride off-gas capture and regeneration on-site — near-empty white space linking streams (a)+(b)+(d).
6. **Biochar-fluorogypsum / fluorspar-tailings composite** for carbon-negative concrete or roadbase — no art combines fluorochemical by-product gypsum with CO2-loaded biochar. [WSU](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/04/230418101419.htm)
7. **Hard-carbon ICE booster** via mineral (Ca/Mg) pre-treatment of lignocellulosic biochar using on-site fluorspar-derived reagents — precursor-doping claims for closed-pore engineering remain contestable. [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566587/)
