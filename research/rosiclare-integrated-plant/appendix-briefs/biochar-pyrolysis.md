## Brief: biochar-pyrolysis

### Key facts
- Slow pyrolysis maximizes solid yield: biochar can reach ~45-50 wt% of dry-ash-free biomass over residence times of minutes to hours, versus fast pyrolysis which favors bio-oil. [ScienceDirect auger review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1537511017302234)
- Fixed carbon rises with peak temperature: hardwood carbon content reached ~89.8% at 700 °C as volatiles devolatilize, but total char yield falls as temperature climbs. Most reactors run 300-550 °C with 5-30 min solid residence (occasionally 25-35 h). [ScienceDirect slow pyrolysis study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589299124000089)
- Hardwood's high lignin content drives higher fixed-carbon biochar than herbaceous feedstocks. [BioResources review](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/recent-advancements-in-biochar-production-according-to-feedstock-classification-pyrolysis-conditions-and-applications-a-review/)
- Activation numbers: physical (steam/CO₂) gives BET ~643 m²/g at ~56.9 wt% yield; chemical (KOH/H₃PO₄) ~783 m²/g at ~75.3 wt% yield. KOH raised one biochar from 91 to 526 m²/g; H₃PO₄ reached 1035-1653 m²/g, sole CO₂ 554-1213 m²/g; the full biochar-to-AC range spans 238-3505 m²/g. [ScienceDirect steam/KOH](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138718110700385X), [BioResources activation](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/effects-of-biomass-type-carbonization-process-and-activation-method-on-the-properties-of-bio-based-activated-carbons/)
- Standards thresholds: IBI rejects any pyrolysis product with <10% organic carbon or molar H/Corg >0.7; EBC requires Corg >50%, H/Corg <0.7, and O/Corg <0.4. [International Biochar Initiative](https://biochar-international.org/biochar-standards/), [EBC v10.5](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)
- CORC biochar credit prices (Nasdaq/Puro CORCCHAR index) clustered ~€125-145/tonne CO₂ in 2025 (€125.42 in Oct 2025) — a 3-5× premium over avoidance credits. First biochar-in-asphalt credits priced above €300/t. [Puro.earth indexes](https://puro.earth/corc-carbon-removal-indexes), [green.earth](https://www.green.earth/news/first-biochar-asphalt-co-removal-credits-priced-above-300/t)
- Market scale: ~3.04 Mt of biochar carbon removal (BCR) credits contracted 2022-2025H1 (1.6 Mt sold in H1 2025); 658 kt delivered since 2022; BCR was 89.4% of durable-CDR delivery volume in Q2 2025. Exomad Green supplied ~60% of BCR credits; Microsoft/Google/BCG/JPMorgan bought ~57%. [CDR.fyi snapshot](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/biochar-carbon-removal-market-snapshot-2025), [CDR.fyi Q2 2025](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/2025-q2-durable-cdr-market-update-biggest-quarter-ever)

### State of the art
Reactor archetypes cluster into (1) continuous auger/screw reactors — tight control of temperature, solid residence, and vapor residence, favored for consistent fixed-carbon char and co-product bio-oil ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1537511017302234)); (2) continuous vertical externally-heated retorts, exemplified by PYREG's PX1500, which runs oxygen-limited pyrolysis at 500-750 °C with syngas combusted in a 950-1050 °C thermal oxidizer for emissions control and heat recovery ([PYREG technology](https://pyreg.com/technology)); (3) rotary kilns (Biochar Now); (4) cyclonic/entrained torrefaction-pyrolysis (Airex CarbonFX, which controls reactor temperature and residence to homogenize biocoal/biochar) ([Airex](https://airex-energy.com/tech/)); and (5) low-capex flame-cap/"Kon-Tiki"/ring kilns (Oregon Kiln: 40 ft³, 14-gauge steel; Ring of Fire ~1.5 yd³ in 3 h) used for in-woods forest-residue conversion at ~$836/tonne marginal cost in field trials ([JoVE flame-cap](https://www.jove.com/t/65543/producing-characterizing-quantifying-biochar-woods-using-portable), [Wilson Biochar](https://wilsonbiochar.com/f/ring-of-fire-biochar-kiln-emissions)). Multi-stage/downdraft Imbert gasifiers (e.g., RESET SyngaSmart) run four-stage ~900 °C processes yielding separated syngas and biochar streams for CHP; biochar itself acts as a catalytic tar-reforming/H₂-boosting medium, and Ni catalysts are the leading tar crackers ([MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3994/5/4/34), [RSC Advances](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/ra/c8ra09045a)). Carbon-credit methodology is maturing fast: Verra VM0044 v1.2 (first project registered Oct 2024), Isometric Biochar Production & Storage v1.3, and CAR's U.S./Canada Biochar methodology were all ICVCM CCP-approved in 2025; Isometric lists ~25 projects (~500k credits expected 2026) ([Verra VM0044](https://verra.org/methodologies/vm0044-biochar-utilization-in-soil-and-non-soil-applications-v1-2/), [Isometric](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biochar/1.3), [ICVCM](https://icvcm.org/biochar/)).

### Gaps & pain points (candidate white space)
- **Tar / bio-oil handling.** Tar is "the most problematic aspect" of gasification for CHP; condensed volatiles on char degrade biochar quality and contaminate it, and cracking requires >1000 °C. White space: integrated in-situ tar-cracking using the plant's own biochar as a sacrificial/regenerable catalyst bed, or a tar-to-fluorochemical-solvent coupling unique to the co-located site. [tracextech](https://tracextech.com/biochar-production-process/), [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3994/5/4/34)
- **Feedstock moisture & drying.** Systems want <15-20% moisture; reactor heat demand is dominated by feed moisture, hurting thermal self-sufficiency. White space: waste-heat-recovery drying loops that reuse thermal-oxidizer/CHP exhaust (ties to the site's syngas-CHP and CaF₂ calcination heat) and moisture-adaptive process control for stable fixed-carbon yield. [ACS self-energized pyrolysis](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.4c03039)
- **Heat integration across streams.** Combusting part of the pyro-gas to sustain reactor temperature is the norm; efficiency hinges on combustion + heat-transfer + feed moisture. White space: shared thermal backbone coupling hardwood-residue pyrolysis waste heat to fluorspar drying/HF-chemistry endothermic steps and CO₂/steam supply for on-site activation. [ScienceDirect biochar review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590123024006881)
- **Activation coupling.** No dominant patent ties on-site CHP-derived CO₂/steam to steam/CO₂ activation of the same hardwood biochar to make >1000 m²/g activated carbon for the site's own fluorochemical scrubbing/HF purification — a defensible integration claim. [ScienceDirect activation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138718110700385X)
- **Emissions permitting & quality control.** Batch/traditional kilns have weak temperature control and higher emissions; continuous reactors need certified low-NOx thermal oxidation for permitting. White space: closed-loop emissions-and-fixed-carbon control (ML/NOx prediction) qualifying char to EBC/IBI + VM0044/Isometric MRV simultaneously. [arXiv NOx ML](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.07881), [Isometric](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biochar/1.3)
- **Carbon-credit stacking.** Century-to-millennia permanence commands the €125-145/t CORC premium; combining biochar-in-concrete/asphalt (>€300/t) with fluorspar-plant use cases is under-patented. [green.earth](https://www.green.earth/news/first-biochar-asphalt-co-removal-credits-priced-above-300/t)
