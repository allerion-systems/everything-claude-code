## Brief: regulatory

### Key facts

- **EPA RMP threshold — HF at 1,000 lb.** "Hydrofluoric acid/Hydrogen fluoride (conc. ≥ 50%)" (CAS 7664-39-3) is a listed toxic substance in Table 1 of 40 CFR 68.130 with a **1,000-lb threshold quantity**; exceeding it in a single process triggers a full Risk Management Program under 40 CFR Part 68 (hazard assessment, worst-case release scenario, offsite consequence analysis, emergency response). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-68/subpart-F/section-68.130 ; EPA guidance on aqueous-HF threshold math (four 55-gal drums of 48% HF ≈ 1,013 lb HF already exceeds TQ): https://www.epa.gov/rmp/calculating-thresholds-toxic-substances-concentration-qualifiers
- **OSHA PSM — HF at 1,000 lb (anhydrous only).** Hydrogen fluoride appears in 29 CFR 1910.119 Appendix A at TQ 1,000 lb; OSHA interpretation letters state PSM covers **anhydrous HF but not aqueous solutions/mixtures**. https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.119AppA ; https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2016-07-21
- **2024 RMP overhaul.** The "Safer Communities by Chemical Accident Prevention" final rule (89 FR 17622, Mar. 11, 2024; effective May 10, 2024) adds Safer Technologies and Alternatives Analysis (STAA) for Program 3 NAICS 324/325 processes (fluorochemical manufacturing is NAICS 325), third-party audits, and STAA practicability assessments specifically flagging HF alkylation. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/11/2024-04458/accidental-release-prevention-requirements-risk-management-programs-under-the-clean-air-act-safer ; EPA proposed rolling parts back in 2025 — compliance target is moving: https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/epa-proposes-rollback-of-chemical-accident-prevention-rules
- **HF is a CAA §112(b) HAP, but there is no source-category NESHAP for HF production.** EPA's AP-42 §8.7 background document describes the standard fluorspar+H2SO4 rotary-kiln HF process and its controls (precondenser, acid/water scrubbers for HF, SiF4, SO2). A new major HAP source (≥10 tpy HF) would face **case-by-case MACT under CAA §112(g)**. https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/documents/final_background_document_for_hydrofluoric_acid_section_8.7.pdf
- **NSPS Subpart OOO covers fluorspar beneficiation.** Fluorspar is one of 18 listed nonmetallic minerals; PM standards apply to each crusher, grinding mill, screen, conveyor, storage bin, and loadout built/modified after Aug. 31, 1983 — with exemptions for facilities in underground mines and wet processing. 40 CFR 60 Subpart OOO: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-60/subpart-OOO
- **Pyrolysis under CAA §129 is unsettled.** EPA's Sept. 8, 2021 ANPRM (86 FR 50296) questioned whether pyrolysis/gasification units are solid-waste incinerators; on June 5, 2023 EPA **withdrew** the 2020 proposal that would have removed "pyrolysis/combustion units" from the OSWI (Other Solid Waste Incineration) definitions, leaving pyrolysis potentially within §129 pending a final OSWI rule. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/09/08/2021-19390/potential-future-regulation-addressing-pyrolysis-and-gasification-units ; https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/05/2023-11476/standards-of-performance-for-new-stationary-sources-and-emission-guidelines-for-existing-sources
- **Bevill exclusion — directly on point.** 40 CFR 261.4(b)(7) excludes extraction/beneficiation wastes broadly, and its closed list of 20 mineral-processing "special wastes" explicitly includes **"fluorogypsum from hydrofluoric acid production" and "process wastewater from hydrofluoric acid production."** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-I/part-261/subpart-A/section-261.4 ; EPA's fluorspar/HF special-waste report: https://archive.epa.gov/epawaste/nonhaz/industrial/special/web/pdf/id4-hfa.pdf
- **Illinois air permitting.** Construction permits are required before building emission units (35 Ill. Adm. Code Parts 201–291); major sources need a CAAPP/Title V permit under 415 ILCS 5/39.5. https://epa.illinois.gov/topics/forms/air-permits.html ; https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=041500050K39.5 ; https://www.epa.gov/caa-permitting/clean-air-act-permitting-illinois
- **Illinois mining law.** IDNR Office of Mines and Minerals (four divisions incl. Land Reclamation and Blasting/Explosives/Aggregate Reclamation) administers the **Fluorspar and Underground Limestone Mines Act (225 ILCS 710)** — a fluorspar-specific safety statute — and the **Surface-Mined Land Conservation and Reclamation Act (225 ILCS 715)** requiring a surface-mining permit + reclamation plan for non-coal operations affecting >10 acres/yr or removing >10 ft of overburden. https://ilga.gov/legislation/ILCS/ilcs3.asp?ActID=1388&ChapterID=24 ; https://dnr.illinois.gov/mines.html ; permit portal: https://dnr2.illinois.gov/OMMLRDePermits/
- **Water.** NPDES effluent limits for fluorspar mines/mills exist as a dedicated subcategory: 40 CFR Part 436 **Subpart K (Fluorspar Subcategory)**, incorporated into IEPA-issued NPDES permits. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-N/part-436/subpart-K ; https://www.epa.gov/eg/mineral-mining-and-processing-effluent-guidelines
- **Biochar permitting precedents.** Minnesota PCA publishes biochar-specific air-permit guidance (synthetic-minor limits to stay under Title V); Monterey Bay ARD issued a first-of-region biochar pyrolysis air permit (Sitos Group, 2024); USFS documents jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction incinerator-definition confusion for biochar units. https://www.pca.state.mn.us/business-with-us/biochar-production ; https://www.sitos.earth/sitos-group-blog/air-permit-for-biochar-technology ; https://research.fs.usda.gov/rmrs/articles/policy-and-regulation-challenges-behind-mobile-biochar-production-us ; https://biochar-us.org/usbi-nabc24-challenges-and-solutions-permitting-pyrolysis-biochar-projects

### State of the art

The compliance template for a US HF-from-fluorspar plant is well established: rotary-kiln acidulation with condenser/scrubber trains (AP-42 §8.7), RMP Program 3 + PSM management systems keyed to the 1,000-lb TQ, Bevill-excluded fluorogypsum stacking, and NPDES under 40 CFR 436. Safety design norms are borrowed from refining: **API RP 751 (4th ed., 2021)** — HF detection, water-mitigation sprays, inventory minimization, 3-year audits, special-emphasis inspection of residual-element carbon steel in HF corrosion zones — is the de facto standard the CSB cites in HF release investigations. https://www.api.org/oil-and-natural-gas/health-and-safety/refinery-and-plant-safety/process-safety/process-safety-standards/rp-751 ; https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/20/pes_final_report_published_october_2022_r1.pdf. Biochar plants, by contrast, are permitted ad hoc as minor/synthetic-minor sources, with the §129 incinerator question unresolved since EPA's 2023 withdrawal. No published norm addresses co-locating RMP-covered HF chemistry with biomass pyrolysis (combustible dust + syngas near a toxic-release process); the closest analog is API RP 751 unit-siting/spacing practice. Reprocessing legacy tailings occupies a genuine gray zone: residues from re-beneficiation of ore/tailings remain within the Bevill extraction/beneficiation exclusion, but adding downstream chemical steps can convert a stream into non-excluded "mineral processing" waste unless it lands on the 20-waste list (fluorogypsum and HF process wastewater do). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-I/part-261/subpart-A/section-261.4

### Gaps & pain points (candidate white space)

1. **Sub-TQ modular HF trains.** Process designs keeping in-process HF inventory below the 1,000-lb RMP/PSM TQ (continuous on-demand generation/consumption, aqueous-HF-only intermediates exploiting OSHA's aqueous exclusion) are patentable safety/architecture inventions with direct compliance value.
2. **Tailings-reprocessing flowsheets engineered to stay Bevill-excluded** — wholly physical/flotation re-beneficiation of legacy Rosiclare/Cave-in-Rock tailings with waste streams that map onto §261.4(b)(7) categories; claims tying process boundaries to waste classification are rare in prior art.
3. **Pyrolysis feed-integrity systems** that document non-waste "clean cellulosic biomass" status (chain-of-custody sensing, contaminant rejection) to keep units outside CAA §129 — valuable while EPA's OSWI rulemaking is pending.
4. **Co-location safety interlocks**: no standard exists for HF units adjacent to biomass dust/syngas systems; inventions adapting API RP 751 water-mitigation and detection to a biochar/HF campus (e.g., biochar-bed HF emergency scrubbing, using site biochar as the sacrificial fluoride sorbent) are open space.
5. **Fluorogypsum + biochar valorization** (solidification/soil products building on US 5,174,972) converts the largest Bevill waste stream into product, addressing the persistent stacking liability.
6. **Permitting-by-design**: integrated CHP/waste-heat flowsheets sized to synthetic-minor caps (Illinois CAAPP avoidance), mirroring the MPCA biochar pathway — defensible as control-system and process-integration claims.
