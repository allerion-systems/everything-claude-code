# Code & Permits Reference

Covers IRC residential deck requirements (2024 IRC, in effect across most U.S. jurisdictions in 2026) plus permitting workflow. Always tell the homeowner to verify with their local building department — codes are amended locally and HOAs can be stricter.

## When a permit is (almost certainly) required

- Deck surface >30" above grade at any point
- Deck attached to the house (any ledger connection)
- Deck >200 sq ft regardless of height (many jurisdictions)
- Any roof, pergola with attached roof, or covered structure
- Hot tub, spa, or built-in cooking appliance on the deck
- Deck in a flood zone, fire zone, or coastal setback overlay
- Permits also typically required: any property with HOA architectural review

## When a permit may not be required

Note: "may not" means **verify before you build**. Common exemptions:
- Freestanding ground-level deck, ≤30" above grade, ≤200 sq ft, not attached to house
- Replacement of existing deck boards only (no structural change)

## Key IRC prescriptive requirements (2024 IRC, chapter R507)

### Live loads
- Residential deck: 40 PSF live load
- Snow load: per local map (varies 0-70 PSF in cold-climate zones)
- Hot tub: design for actual unit weight (water-filled) + 100 PSF occupancy

### Joists (R507.6)

Joist size based on span (PT SYP #2, 16" o.c., 40 PSF live + 10 PSF dead):

| Span | Joist size |
|------|-----------|
| ≤6 ft | 2×6 |
| 6-10 ft | 2×8 |
| 10-14 ft | 2×10 |
| 14-18 ft | 2×12 |
| >18 ft | Engineered (LVL or steel) |

Cantilever max = ¼ of backspan, not >24" without engineer.

### Beams (R507.5)

Beam size based on tributary width (the joist span the beam carries) and post spacing. See table in `estimator-formulas.md`. Use double or triple 2×10 / 2×12 PT, or LVL for long spans.

### Posts (R507.4)

- 4×4 PT acceptable to 8 ft height for residential decks ≤2 stories
- 6×6 PT GC required >8 ft or for any deck supporting a roof
- Post-to-beam connection: positive mechanical connection (Simpson PC, BC, or equivalent)
- Post-to-footing: post base anchor (Simpson ABU or equivalent) — do not bury PT post in concrete

### Footings (R507.3)

- Min diameter based on tributary area and soil bearing (1,500 PSF default)
- Footing bottom must be below frost line (varies — see below)
- Concrete: 2,500 PSI minimum
- Pad/spread footings allowed but require sizing per table

### Ledger (R507.9)

- Direct attachment to band joist (rim joist) of house framing
- 1/2" hex bolts or structural screws, staggered top/bottom, max 16" o.c.
- Flashing required at ledger
- Lateral load connection (R507.9.2) required:
  - Two 1,500-lb hold-down devices on opposing joists, OR
  - 4× hold-down devices (750 lb each) per IRC alternate
- Do NOT ledger to brick veneer, EIFS, stucco, or unknown — go freestanding

### Guardrails (R312)

- Required when walking surface is >30" above grade or adjacent surface
- Height: 36" minimum (residential)
- Opening size: must not allow a 4" sphere to pass (4" max gap between balusters)
- Triangular openings at stairs: must not allow 6" sphere to pass
- Strength: must resist 200-lb point load at top rail in any direction
- Stair railing must run continuously alongside stairs at 34-38" measured from stair nosing

### Stairs (R311.7)

- Riser height: 4" min, 7-3/4" max; all risers in a flight within 3/8" of each other
- Tread depth: 10" min (measured nose to nose); all treads within 3/8" of each other
- Width: 36" min for residential
- Headroom: 6'-8" min above stair nosing
- Handrail: 34-38" above stair nosing, both sides if width >44"
- Landing required at top and bottom of run; 36" × stair width minimum
- Max riser run before landing required: 12 risers

Always verify with the local building department. Frost depth governs footing depth absolutely — a footing above frost line will heave and crack the deck.

## Setbacks

Typical residential setbacks for an open deck (no roof):

| Type of property line | Typical setback |
|------------------------|------------------|
| Front | 20-40 ft (often defined by house, not deck) |
| Side | 5-10 ft |
| Rear | 10-25 ft |
| Easement (utility, drainage) | No structures within easement |

Decks <30" above grade are sometimes exempt from rear/side setback in some jurisdictions. HOAs often impose stricter (and material-specific) rules.

## 811 / Miss Utility

Federal law in U.S. — call 811 at least 48 hours (typically 2-3 business days) before any digging, including footings. Free service. Each utility (electric, gas, water, sewer, telecom, cable) sends out a locator to mark their lines. Hitting an unmarked line: still on you. Hitting a marked line: their problem. Hitting an unmarked one if you didn't call: serious fines plus repair cost.

## Permit application — what to bring

1. **Plot plan** — bird's-eye view of property with house, lot lines, easements, and proposed deck location dimensioned from property lines
2. **Framing plan** — top-down view of joists, beams, posts, footings with sizes and spans noted
3. **Elevation drawing** — side view showing deck height, railing height, stair detail
4. **Cross-section** — showing ledger detail, post-to-beam connection, footing depth
5. **Material list** — what you're building with (lumber grade, fasteners)
6. **Contractor license number** (if applicable) and proof of liability insurance
7. **Permit fee** — $100-1,200 depending on jurisdiction and deck complexity

Decks.com plans (sold via the deck plans page) include items 2-5 in the package. Custom designs typically need a drafter or architect; expect $400-1,500 for sealed/stamped plans if your jurisdiction requires them.

## Inspections (typical)

| Inspection | When | What's checked |
|------------|------|----------------|
| Footing | After footings poured, before backfill | Depth, diameter, rebar (if required), concrete cure |
| Framing | After framing complete, before decking | Joist sizing/spacing, ledger attachment, beam/post connections, hurricane ties |
| Final | After decking, railing, stairs complete | Railing height, baluster spacing, stair dimensions, lateral load device |

A failed inspection is not catastrophic — fix it and re-inspect. Most jurisdictions don't charge for the first re-inspect.

## Risk flags to surface to the homeowner

Always call these out before quoting:

1. **Deck >30" above grade**: guardrail required, permit required
2. **Deck >8 ft above grade**: structural engineer recommended
3. **Ledger to anything other than wood-framed wall**: freestanding default
4. **Cantilever >24"**: engineering required
5. **Hot tub, spa, or large planter (>500 lb)**: tributary load calc needed
6. **Within 5 ft of property line**: setback risk; check zoning before design
7. **Within easement**: cannot build; relocate or apply for variance
8. **Existing deck older than 15 years**: assume ledger flashing missing, joists may show rot — budget tear-off
9. **Steep slope (>3:1) within 10 ft of footings**: foundation/soil engineering may be required
10. **Coastal or flood zone**: special anchoring and elevation rules apply (FEMA + local)
