# Site Photo Analysis Checklist

Use this checklist for every photo the homeowner uploads. Pull as much as possible from the image; explicitly mark anything you cannot determine.

## 1. House style & era

Identify the architectural style — this drives railing/feature choices.

| Style cue in photo | House style |
|--------------------|-------------|
| Symmetric facade, shutters, brick or clapboard | Colonial / traditional |
| Single-story, low pitch, attached garage, long horizontal lines | Ranch |
| Exposed rafter tails, porch columns on stone bases, low-pitch gable | Craftsman |
| Flat or low-slope roof, large glass, metal accents | Modern / contemporary |
| Two levels offset, half-flight stair inside | Split-level |
| Stone/stucco, arched openings, terracotta tile | Mediterranean |
| Board-and-batten, metal roof, simple gable | Modern farmhouse |

## 2. Siding & attachment substrate

Critical for determining whether the deck can attach via a ledger or must be freestanding.

| What you see | Attachment guidance |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Vinyl lap siding | Ledger OK after removing siding in ledger zone and flashing properly |
| Fiber-cement (Hardie) | Ledger OK; same flashing requirements |
| Wood lap / cedar shingle | Ledger OK; inspect band joist for rot |
| Brick veneer | **Do NOT ledger to veneer.** Either freestanding deck OR engineered through-bolts into framed wall behind veneer (engineer required) |
| Stucco / EIFS | Ledger risky — water intrusion history. Default to freestanding |
| Stone veneer | Same as brick — freestanding default |
| Log home | Specialty fasteners; refer to log home manual |

## 3. Door type & threshold height

The door determines the deck's finished surface elevation.

- **Patio slider / French doors**: target deck surface ~1" below interior floor to keep water out.
- **Single in-swing door with sill**: target ~1.5" drop from threshold to deck.
- **Walk-out basement door**: deck will be elevated; expect stairs.

Estimate height above grade by:
- Counting siding courses (vinyl/wood lap ~4"–5" exposure each; brick courses ~2-2/3" each).
- Comparing to a known reference (a standard exterior door is 80" tall; a standard step is 7-3/4").
- Asking the user if it's ambiguous.

Bucket the height:
- **<30" above grade**: no guardrail required by IRC, often no permit (verify locally).
- **30–48"**: guardrail required at 36" minimum height.
- **>48"**: guardrail at 42" min in many jurisdictions; engineer review recommended.
- **>8 ft**: engineer required; lateral bracing matters.

## 4. Available wall width

The deck's long dimension is usually constrained by the wall.

Estimate available width by:
- Counting siding courses or window widths (standard double-hung window is ~32–36" wide).
- Identifying the boundaries: where does the wall stop? Where are corners, bump-outs, chimneys?

Note any obstacles in the attachment zone:
- Windows below 30" above finished deck height become guardrail violations.
- Dryer vents, gas meter regulators, hose bibs in the ledger zone need relocation or design-around.

## 5. Grade & drainage

This is where DIY deck plans go wrong.

| What you see | Implication |
|--------------|-------------|
| Flat ground, no visible water staining | Simple footings, standard post heights |
| Gentle slope (1-3" across 10 ft) away from house | Ideal — drainage is already working |
| Gentle slope toward house | Drainage problem — deck must not trap water; consider gravel + drain |
| Steep slope (>6" per 10 ft) | Multi-level deck or stepped footings; post heights vary |
| Visible pooling, moss, dark soil | Drainage fix required before deck; otherwise frost heave & rot |
| Downspout terminating in deck zone | Reroute downspout before building |
| Concrete patio in place | Build over it (sleepers + decking) OR demo it (add $500-2000) |

## 6. Sun exposure

Read shadows in the photos and ask if unclear. Drives material choice.

- **Full afternoon sun (west/southwest)**: dark composite gets very hot (>140°F surface); recommend lighter colors or hardwood.
- **Mostly shade**: any material works; mildew risk on composite — choose capped.
- **Morning sun only**: most materials fine.

## 7. Obstacles & utilities within 10 ft

Inventory each, with approximate distance:

- AC condenser — needs 24" clearance per manufacturer; do not build over
- Gas meter — typically 36" clearance per local code; do not enclose
- Electrical meter / service entrance — do not block access
- Dryer vent — needs 36" clearance from any combustible
- Hose bibs — keep accessible or relocate
- Septic tank / leach field — do not build over; check property survey
- Trees within 10 ft — root protection zone is roughly 1 ft radius per 1" trunk diameter
- Existing fence — note distance for setback calc
- Overhead power lines — 10 ft clearance minimum from any deck surface

## 8. Property line & setbacks

If a fence or neighbor's house is visible, estimate distance.

- Typical residential setback: 5-10 ft from side/rear property line for decks >30" high.
- Decks <30" high are sometimes exempt — verify with local jurisdiction.
- HOAs may impose stricter setbacks and material restrictions.

## 9. Existing deck (if remodel)

If the photo shows an existing deck being replaced:

- Note size, shape, elevation, and material.
- Look for rot signs: dark staining at ledger, soft fascia, cupped/cracked boards.
- Decide: tear off entirely vs. reuse framing (rare — framing is usually past life).
- Note disposal: 200 sq ft of tear-off ≈ one 10-yard dumpster ≈ $400-700.

## 10. What you couldn't determine

Always include this section in the Site Analysis output. Examples:

- "I can't see the door threshold from this angle — can you measure from the interior floor down to the ground outside?"
- "The wall behind the deck zone is partially blocked by the existing grill — is the siding vinyl or fiber-cement?"
- "I can't tell whether your soil is clay or sandy — if you're east of the Mississippi and on a slope, assume clay and plan footings to local frost depth."

## Output format

Combine all findings into one Markdown report (see SKILL.md Step 2). Be concrete: give numbers in feet/inches, not "small" or "large".
